The Spread That Closed

In mid-2013 a Nepali commercial bank charged its borrowers points more than it paid its depositors. The gap is now . It halved while both prices collapsed — and hardly any of the halving happened during the collapse.

Read this first

There is no series. There is a stack of PDFs

Nepal Rastra Bank publishes one typed monthly snapshot of the banking system and nothing else. No CSV, no API, no table anyone can download. The URL that looks like a page serves application/pdf. Everything on this page was read back out of that prose.

English bulletins are listed in the bank’s sitemaps. of them come back as real PDFs with a text layer. Of those, state the weighted-average deposit and lending rates of commercial banks in a form this page could read. never state them at all, and use the words but print a number the parser would have had to guess at, so they were dropped. That is the whole accounting. Nothing was filled in from anywhere else.

The wording moved constantly. The same fact is written as “the weighted average deposit rate of commercial banks stood at 6.49 percent and lending rate at 12.47 percent” in one year and as “weighted average deposit rate and lending rate of commercial banks stood at 6.93 percent and 10.60 percent respectively” in another, and after 2022 as one limb of a sentence that also covers development banks and finance companies. On top of that the text layer sprays spaces through words and numbers — Deposit s, com mercial, 5. 96 percent, 1 0.55 percent — so every phrase the parser looks for is matched with any amount of whitespace allowed between any two characters, and every number is closed up before it is read.

The series is checked against itself. Each bulletin restates both rates as they stood twelve months earlier. That restatement is never used to fill a month; it is used to test one. months can be tested this way, and in all the figure parsed from the bulletin a year earlier matches the figure the later bulletin says it was, to the last printed digit. The build refuses to write this page if even one disagrees.

The hole in the middle is not a download failure. Between and — months — the bulletins stop printing the two rates and print a single “interest rate spread” instead, computed to the bank’s own directive. issues do this. That number is not lending minus deposit and it is not drawn here, because putting it on the same line would be inventing a quantity the archive never published.

The limitation that matters

A weighted average is not a price anyone was charged

Every number on this page is a weighted average across all commercial banks. It is the system’s mean, weighted by the size of the loans and deposits behind it. No borrower was ever offered it.

A bank lends to a hydropower company at one rate and to a shopkeeper in Birgunj at another. Some of the spread in the figures below is the cost of running a bank; some of it is the difference between those two customers. The bulletin collapses all of it into one number a month, and it publishes nothing about the shape underneath — no range, no quartiles, no breakdown by borrower, no distribution across banks.

So this page cannot show you the spread any individual borrower faced, and it does not try. That is not a modelling choice. The archive never published it. A dispersion figure here would have to be invented, and a made-up range around a real average is worse than no range at all, because it looks like evidence.

Read everything below as a statement about the banking system in aggregate. It is a real quantity and it moves for real reasons. It is simply not the quantity a person walking into a branch is quoted.

Pass-through

A point on deposits is a point and a bit on loans

Fit the twelve-month change in the lending rate against the twelve-month change in the deposit rate, over every pair of months the archive supplies both ends for, and the line is almost the data. Slope , correlation , over overlapping years.

A slope above one means the lending rate moves further than the deposit rate in both directions: the spread widens when money is dear and narrows when it is cheap. The intercept says the rest. With the deposit rate unchanged over a year, the lending rate still falls points. That drift is the closing, and it is running underneath everything else in this archive.

What is not here

Three things the archive will not support

The dispersion. Stated once already and worth stating again, because it is the limit that matters most on this page. These are weighted averages. The spread a particular borrower pays is not in the archive in any month, in any issue, in any form.

The interbank rate. It is in these bulletins and it would have made the liquidity cycle visible directly. It is not drawn here because the definition changes underneath it: the earlier issues report a rate among commercial banks and a separate one among other financial institutions, and the later issues report one rate among all banks and financial institutions. Splicing the two would put a step in the line that no bank ever felt, and there is nothing in the archive that converts between them.

Anything before . The bulletins go back further, and they are in the archive, but they report short-term rates — treasury bills, interbank, refinance — and not the deposit and lending rates of the banking system. The series starts where the publishing does.
