Four things that bound every number above

The survey measures spending, not earning. Nepal's living standards survey records what households consume, because in an economy where much of the income is a farm, a shop and money sent from the Gulf, consumption is the thing that can actually be measured. Your salary has been treated as if all of it were spent. A household that saves heavily will show up lower on this curve than its income alone would suggest.

It is priced in 2022 rupees. A wage quoted today buys less, so today's figure is deflated by — before the lookup. Skip that step, as most calculators do, and everyone is flattered by several percentile points.

The country is not one place. Per-person consumption in the Kathmandu Valley runs about — a month against about — in Madhesh. A single national curve puts a modest Kathmandu household high and a comfortable Madhesh household higher still, and neither would recognise the description.

The tail is thin and the top is missing. Household surveys systematically fail to reach the very rich, who decline them and whose income is hardest to measure. Everything above about the 99th percentile here should be read as "very high" and not as a number.
