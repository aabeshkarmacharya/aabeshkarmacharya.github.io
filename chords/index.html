Nepali songs, by their chords

Every song here is written down as numerals (I, IV, vi, V) rather than as chord names. That is what lets the page play it in any key, find the capo that turns barre chords into open ones without changing a note you hear, and notice when two songs you never connected are built from the same four moves.

Shapes you need

Songs that share a progression

Grouped by the numerals, so a song in G and a song in E land side by side when they make the same moves. A loop counts once whichever chord it starts on (I–IV and IV–I are the same two chords rocking back and forth), and colour chords like add9 and 7 are ignored: Cadd9 is a way of voicing C, not a different step.

What can I play already?

Type the chords you can play. The page tries every capo from 0 to – and lists the songs you can get through at the recording's pitch using only those shapes.

Separate with spaces or commas. Add9, sus and 6 chords count as played if you know the plain chord they are built on.
