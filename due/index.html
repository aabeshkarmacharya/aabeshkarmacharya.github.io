Is Nepal due for a great earthquake?

India slides under Nepal about two centimetres a year, and the fault between them is stuck. This works out how much slip each stretch of it owes since it last broke, what that does to the stress on it, and the chance it breaks within a span of years you choose. Every assumption is on a slider, because none of them is known well enough to hide.

Stress, building

The load since the fault last broke

The line is slip the fault owes: the plates keep moving and the locked fault does not, so the debt grows at the convergence rate, every year, without pause. The band is how much slip great Himalayan ruptures have released. Where the line enters the band, a rupture of the familiar size becomes possible, not scheduled.

The odds rise, and then stop rising

Chance in the next 50 years, by years already waited

Solid: this page's answer, averaged over the uncertainty. Dashed: the same model with every assumption fixed at its middle value. Dotted: a fault with no memory at all.

A locked fault ought to get more dangerous every year it holds, and for a while the model agrees: straight after a rupture the chance of another is close to nothing, and it climbs as the load does.

Then it levels off. The model does not know how much slip this fault can hold, only a range, and every silent year is evidence about which part of the range is right. A fault that has waited 521 years is more likely to be one that normally waits that long, so the silence pushes the estimated interval out from – to – years, and the rising load and the lengthening estimate cancel.

What the catalogue says

Small earthquakes cannot pay for great ones

The instrumental record is too short to contain a single great Himalayan rupture, so it cannot be the forecast. It can still test one. If the steady rain of small and moderate earthquakes were releasing the load, there would be nothing owed.

A probability, not a prediction

Nobody can say when. No method has ever predicted the date of a large earthquake, and this is not one. What it gives is the chance over a span of decades, which is what building codes and insurers use. Kathmandu was called overdue for years before 2015; that was right about the kind of earthquake and the region, and could not have said April.

The answer is mostly assumptions, and they are on sliders. The slip a great rupture releases, the length of each stretch, and how regularly the fault breaks are each known to perhaps a factor of two. The segment lengths are rounded hard. How far east the 1505 rupture ran is argued over, and if it stopped short, part of western Nepal has been loading for longer still.

The stress figure is an order of magnitude. It divides the slip owed by the width of the locked fault, which is the simplest physics that gives the right size; a real stress model needs the fault's shape and the rock around it. Its use here is to show the load is in the range where great ruptures have happened, not to say exactly where in it.

Great earthquakes are not the only danger. This forecasts the rare ruptures that break a whole stretch of the fault. A magnitude 6 or 7 can happen anywhere in Nepal in any year, and the catalogue says one of magnitude 7 happens somewhere in this box about every 59 years.

Nothing here is about your building. How hard the ground moves under a house depends on the rupture, the distance and the soil, and what happens next depends on how the house was built. That is the question worth spending money on.
